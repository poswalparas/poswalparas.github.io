Abstract
In this study, a partially surface fluorinated 2-Pmmn borophene has been proposed as stable 2D structure to explore the emerging magnetic and electronic characteristics induced by 3d transition metal adsorption. Amongst functionalized borophene (B8F4)’s α-, β- and γ-configurations, the γ-B8F4 identifies as energetically favorable and dynamically stable structure due to the lowest formation energy (−89.89 eV) and having no imaginary phonon modes. Adsorption of 3d transition metal atoms (V, Cr, Mn, Fe and Co) at the most favorable site on γ-B8F4, significantly induce the magnetic ordering in nonmagnetic γ-B8F4. Followed by higher degree of spin asymmetry, Mn-adsorbed γ-B8F4 structure leads to maximum magnetic moment (4.87 μB). Spin-orbit coupling enables calculation utilizing force field theorem, which reveals magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE = 1.681 meV) in Mn-adsorbed γ- B8F4 structure, demonstrating anisotropy-driven stability of 2D magnetism and defining an out-of-plane easy axis for Mn adsorption. Furthermore, Monte-Carlo simulation based on Heisenberg model estimates that Co-adsorbed γ-B8F4 structure exhibits ferromagnetic ordering with significant curie temperature (34.5 K). These findings establish fluorinated borophene as a stable, adaptable 2D magnetic material platform, providing an efficient pathway toward engineering room-temperature ferromagnetism for potential spintronic applications.
